🛡️ Flood Safety Guide

General flood safety information compiled for educational purposes. Always follow instructions from official emergency services in your area.

📢
Important Disclaimer
This guide provides general awareness information only. Emergency telephone numbers, authority contact details, and official evacuation routes are not listed here because they vary by location. Please obtain this information from your local District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA), State Emergency Operations Centre, or equivalent local authority.
🏠

Before a Flood

  • 📦
    Prepare an emergency kit

    Keep at least 3 days' supply of drinking water (at least 1 litre per person per day), non-perishable food, medicines, torches, batteries, first-aid supplies, and important documents in a waterproof bag.

  • 🗺️
    Know your evacuation routes

    Identify the nearest high ground, evacuation shelters, and at least two routes out of your area. Share this information with all household members, including children and elderly relatives.

  • 📞
    Identify emergency contacts

    Write down local emergency service numbers and keep them on paper (not just in your phone). Contact your local government office or DDMA for the correct numbers in your district.

  • 🏡
    Protect your home

    Move valuable items, electrical appliances, and important documents to upper floors. Ensure that drainage channels near your property are kept clear of blockages.

  • 📻
    Stay informed

    Monitor official weather bulletins and flood warnings from your local government, state meteorological department, or national disaster management authority. A battery-powered or hand-crank radio can be invaluable when power is lost.

  • 🐄
    Plan for animals and livestock

    Make arrangements in advance for pets and livestock. Know the nearest animal shelter or higher ground that can accommodate them.

🌊

During a Flood

  • 🏔️
    Move to higher ground immediately

    If authorities issue an evacuation order or if water is rising rapidly, move to higher ground without delay. Do not wait to gather all belongings — your life is more important.

  • 🚫
    Never walk or drive through floodwater

    Just 15 cm (6 inches) of moving water can knock a person down. 30 cm (1 foot) of water can sweep away a small vehicle. Floodwater may hide open drains, damaged roads, and submerged debris.

  • ⚡
    Avoid contact with floodwater if possible

    Floodwater can carry sewage, chemicals, and debris. If you must wade through it, wash and disinfect your skin thoroughly afterwards. Do not touch electrical equipment or power lines in or near floodwater.

  • 🏠
    If trapped indoors, go upstairs

    Move to the highest level of your building. Signal for help from a window using a bright cloth or torch. Do not enter the attic unless you have a way to escape onto the roof if water continues to rise.

  • 📵
    Use phones for emergencies only

    Conserve your phone battery for essential emergency calls. Avoid posting on social media until you are safe. Keep your phone in a sealed bag to protect it from water.

  • 🧃
    Drink only safe water

    Tap water may be contaminated during flooding. Use stored bottled water or boil water before drinking if you are unsure. Do not use water from flooded wells until it has been tested and declared safe.

🌤️

After a Flood

  • 🔒
    Return home only when declared safe

    Wait for official confirmation from authorities that it is safe to return. Returning too early may expose you to structural hazards, contaminated water, and continuing flood risks.

  • 🔍
    Check for structural damage before entering

    Look for cracks in foundations, walls, and ceilings. If your home appears structurally unsafe, do not enter and contact the relevant authority for an inspection.

  • 🧹
    Clean and disinfect your home

    Remove mud and debris carefully. Wash all surfaces that came into contact with floodwater using clean water and disinfectant. Discard any food items that may have been contaminated.

  • 💡
    Check electrical and gas systems

    Do not switch on electrical appliances or gas systems until a qualified technician has inspected them for flood damage. Keep the power off at the mains until this inspection is complete.

  • 🩺
    Watch for health problems

    Contact with floodwater can lead to skin infections, gastrointestinal illness, and exposure to hazardous chemicals. Seek medical attention if you or a family member develops unusual symptoms after flood exposure.

  • 🤝
    Support your community

    Check on elderly neighbours and vulnerable individuals in your area. Report damage and hazards to local authorities so that resources can be directed where they are most needed.

ℹ️
About This Guide
This safety information is drawn from general flood preparedness best practices. It has not been verified or endorsed by any government authority. Individual situations vary significantly — always follow the specific instructions of your local disaster management and emergency services teams.
